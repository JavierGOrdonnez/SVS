# The Nested Breakdown: CSA → CSE → Group-Based → "Grooming Gangs"

**Purpose**: Precisely separate the categories the public conversation conflates  
**Data**: England & Wales police-recorded crime, 2024 (VKPP) + Casey Audit (2025) + COCAD  
**Branch**: `research/uk-grooming-policy-analysis`

---

## Why This Matters

The public debate uses "grooming gangs," "CSE," and "child sexual abuse" interchangeably. They are not the same thing. They are **nested categories** — each a small subset of the one above it. Conflating them inflates the apparent scale of the specific phenomenon (organised grooming networks) by a factor of roughly 175x.

This document separates them precisely, with the caveat (critical, see §6) that all figures are *recorded* offences and under-reporting is severe.

---

## Part 1: The Definitions (Official, Nested)

### CSA — Child Sexual Abuse (the umbrella)
Any situation where someone under 18 is coerced, manipulated, or forced into sexual activity. Includes **contact** abuse (assault, rape) and **non-contact** abuse (exposure to images, online). This is the broadest category.

### CSE — Child Sexual Exploitation (a subset of CSA)
[CSE is explicitly *"a form of child sexual abuse"*](https://learning.nspcc.org.uk/child-abuse-and-neglect/child-sexual-exploitation). The distinguishing feature: **an exchange**. The child — or someone else — receives *something* in return for the sexual activity (money, drugs, gifts, accommodation, status, "affection"), or the perpetrator gains financial advantage/status. **All CSE is CSA; most CSA is not CSE.**

### Group-Based CSE (a subset of CSE)
CSE involving **two or more perpetrators** acting together. Critically, [this spans a vast range](https://www.vkpp.org.uk/vkpp-work/cse-task/): *"from unorganised peer group sharing of imagery, to more organised complex high-harm cases with high community impact."* Two 15-year-olds sharing an image and a 30-man trafficking network are **both** "group-based."

### "Grooming Gangs" (a subset of group-based CSE)
**Not an official category.** [The Casey Audit states explicitly](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-audit-on-group-based-child-sexual-exploitation-and-abuse) that "grooming gangs," though used in public and media, *"is not captured clearly in any official data set."* The closest official proxy is **"complex and organised"** abuse recorded in the COCAD dataset (Complex and Organised Child Abuse Database). This is the Rotherham/Telford archetype: organised networks, multiple offenders, multiple victims, systematic targeting.

## Part 3: The Same Data as a Table

| Level | Category | Recorded offences (yr) | % of all recorded CSAE | What it is |
|-------|----------|------------------------|------------------------|------------|
| 1 | **All CSAE** | 122,768 (2024) | 100% | Every recorded child sexual offence |
| 2 | **CSE** (exploitation) | ~17,000 (2024) | ~14% | Subset of CSA with an *exchange* element |
| 3 | **Group-based** offending | ~4,228 (2024) | ~3.7% | 2+ perpetrators; mostly *pairs* |
| 4 | **Organised networks** ("grooming gangs") | ~700 (2023) | ~0.6% | Complex/organised; the Rotherham archetype |

**The headline**: The phenomenon dominating national political debate ("grooming gangs") accounts for roughly **0.6% of recorded child sexual abuse** — about **1 in every 175 recorded offences**. And even that 0.6% includes organised networks of *all* ethnicities, not only the ethnically-homogenous street-grooming cases that drive the media narrative.

## Part 4: Reading the Funnel — Where Each "Common Belief" Sits

### "White perpetrators are lone wolves; gangs are the migrant problem"
This belief maps two real categories onto an ethnic split that the data doesn't support:
- **Individual CSE/CSA** (the vast majority — Levels 1–2 minus group-based): perpetrators of *all* ethnicities, predominantly white, predominantly known to the victim (family/friend/acquaintance).
- **Group-based offending** (Level 3): also predominantly *pairs*, also ethnically mixed. Three-quarters are two-perpetrator cases, not gangs.
- **Organised networks** (Level 4, ~700): this is where the high-profile ethnically-homogenous cases sit — but it is the *smallest* tier, and even here organised white networks exist (they're simply not labelled "grooming gangs").

The "lone white / gang migrant" framing collapses once you see that **individual perpetration and group perpetration both occur across all ethnicities**, and the organised-network tier where Asian over-representation is documented regionally is ~0.6% of the whole.

### "CSE is huge and growing"
CSA/CSAE is huge and growing (122,768; +6% YoY) — but the **growth is overwhelmingly online and non-contact** (+26% online, +17% non-contact), and ~50% of *all* CSAE is committed by other children. The organised-network tier is not what's driving the totals.

## Part 5: What This Does to the "More Migrants" Argument

Even granting every regional over-representation claim, migration-restriction policy could *only* touch a fraction of Level 4 (~700 organised-network offences), because:

- **Levels 1–2 (the ~96–99% majority)** are individual perpetrators, family members, and other children — untouched by migration policy.
- **Level 3** is mostly pairs (siblings, peers, couples) — untouched.
- **Level 4** is the only tier where the "grooming gang" image lives, and:
  - It is ~0.6% of recorded CSAE.
  - Its organised networks include white-British networks too.
  - Most documented perpetrators in the high-profile cases were **British-born or long-resident** (migration policy is retroactively irrelevant to them).

So the maximum theoretical "reachable" share of the problem via migration policy is a fraction of 0.6% — while the institutional reforms (Levels 1–4 alike) address all 122,768 recorded offences and the ~380,000 estimated unreported ones.

---

## Part 6: The Critical Caveat — Under-Reporting and Data Quality

I am presenting **recorded** proportions. Two honest caveats cut against over-reading them:

1. **Severe under-reporting (all tiers).** Only ~1 in 5 CSA incidents is reported. The true denominator (~500,000/yr) dwarfs the 122,768 recorded. We do **not** know the true proportions of any tier.

2. **The "grooming gang" tier may be *especially* hidden.** Organised exploitation deliberately conceals victims, who are often disbelieved or afraid. So the recorded ~700 / 0.6% could *understate* the true organised-network share. [The Casey Audit itself warns](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-audit-on-group-based-child-sexual-exploitation-and-abuse) it is *"highly unlikely that this accurately reflects the true scale"* — definitions are inconsistently applied and the data is poor.

**The intellectually honest position**: Of what is *measured*, organised grooming networks are a small recorded fraction (~0.6%) of child sexual abuse. The true figure is unknown and could be higher. But this uncertainty cuts **both ways** and does not rescue the migration-causation argument, because:
- The same data gaps (especially missing ethnicity and *zero* immigration-status recording) make any *targeted* policy unbuildable.
- Whatever the true scale of the organised-network tier, institutional reform (believe victims, share intelligence, resource investigations, prosecute) is the lever that surfaces and stops it — and works identically regardless of perpetrator origin.

---

## Part 7: The One-Paragraph Summary

> "Child sexual abuse (CSA) is the umbrella — ~123,000 offences recorded in England & Wales in 2024, but likely ~500,000 actual. CSE (exploitation, defined by an *exchange*) is a subset of that — about 17,000, or ~14% of recorded cases. Group-based CSE (2+ perpetrators) is a subset of *that* — about 4,228, or ~3.7% — and three-quarters of those are just *two* people, not gangs. The 'grooming gangs' the debate is about — organised, complex networks — are the smallest tier: about 700 recorded offences in 2023, roughly 0.6% of all recorded child sexual abuse. The data is incomplete and under-reporting is severe, so the true figure is unknown — but that uncertainty also means you can't build a precise migration policy on it, while institutional reform addresses every tier at once."
