# Source: From "migrant-crime" discourse to hate speech to real-world aggression — Spain

**Used for:** Context/catalogue doc — not a numeric data table. Supports any future SPEC task on hate-speech/hate-crime as an *outcome* variable (distinct from T26-T29's "migrant crime rate" work, which treats migrants as possible *perpetrators*). Mirrors the role `fuentes_secundarias_analisis_espana.md` plays for sexual violence: secondary analyses + catalogue, not a primary statistical series.

**Scope of the question:** how does crime-framed migration discourse (political, media, social-media) escalate into hate speech, and how does that hate speech translate into real aggression/violence in Spain — historically and currently.

---

## 1. The mechanism, as documented in the literature

Three actors recur across every source below: (1) a **trigger event** — usually a real crime, sometimes a fabricated or unrelated one — involving a person of immigrant origin; (2) **rapid online amplification** that reframes the individual act as evidence about the whole group, frequently using disinformation (fabricated photos, out-of-context video, fake "suspect lists"); (3) **organized offline mobilization** ("cacerías"/hunts, patrols, riots) usually led by far-right—adjacent groups, often with participants traveling in from outside the affected town.

- OBERAXE (Spain's official racism/xenophobia observatory) found that **43% of racist/xenophobic hate-speech posts originate from "citizen security" (crime) incidents**, and that in Jul–Aug 2024 **more than half of hate speech on social media was based on false information or rumours** — i.e. the crime-narrative trigger is empirically the dominant pathway into hate speech, and it is disinformation-heavy by default. [Source: La Moncloa/OBERAXE press notes, 2024-2025, linked below]
- Heidi Beirich (Global Project Against Hate and Extremism, GPAHE) on the Spanish case, Sept. 2025: "It's no longer limited to discourse, [it] translates into concrete violent actions that generate fear in the Muslim community" — GPAHE's report frames Vox's normalization of anti-immigrant/Islamophobic rhetoric as the permissive condition that licenses "ordinary" actors (not just organized extremist cadres) to act. [Infobae, 2025-09-03, citing GPAHE report]
- Academic corpus analysis of Vox's Twitter/X output (peer-reviewed, Social Sciences journal, 2022) documents systematic Islamophobic hate speech as a deliberate party strategy, not incidental rhetoric.
- A 2025 study in *Javnost – The Public* on far-right political influencers in Spain finds their hate-speech/divisive content is built to foster "performative polarisation" that **explicitly aims at mobilization**, not just persuasion — i.e. the rhetoric is designed with action in mind.
- International comparative framing (cited in Spanish press analysing Torre Pacheco): the same script — trigger → online "hunt" call → street violence — recurs in Chemnitz (Germany), Southport (UK, 2024), Ballymena (Northern Ireland, 2025), and now repeatedly within Spain. Far-right Spanish influencers explicitly cited the 2024 English riots as a model to replicate domestically (summer 2024, pre-Torre Pacheco).

**Caveat:** the *correlation* between political/media discourse and hate-crime levels is well documented; *strict causal* identification is harder. One Spanish province-level study (Soc. Sci. 2022) testing whether online hate-speech volume predicts immigration-acceptance attitudes at the provincial level found **no significant correlation** at that level of aggregation — a reminder that the mechanism operates at the event/mobilization level (sudden spikes tied to a trigger), not as a smooth background correlation visible in cross-sectional attitude data.

- New in 2024: Islamophobia reported as its own sub-category for the first time — 13 cases.

### 2.3bis Dark figure / underreporting of hate crimes, incl. migrant-specific barriers

**Headline figure:** Spain's own official victim survey finds only **10.76% of hate-crime victims report to police** — i.e. an **~89% dark figure**, among the highest documented for any crime category in this repo (cf. `macroencuesta.md`'s sexual-violence dark-figure rates, which are lower).

- **Source:** "Infradenuncia de los Delitos de Odio en España: ¿Por qué es tan alta?" — joint study by ONDOD (Oficina Nacional para los Delitos de Odio, Ministerio del Interior), Universidad Pontificia Comillas and Universidad de Salamanca (incl. OBERAXE director Tomás Fernández), based on a survey of ~800 respondents fielded Dec 2020–Mar 2021. Since 2025, Interior now runs this as a recurring "Encuesta sobre delitos de odio" (victims/witnesses/general population), precisely to track the dark figure year over year rather than as a one-off.
- **By severity:** lower-intensity acts (insults, degrading treatment, everyday discrimination) are disproportionately normalized/absorbed privately by victims and almost never reported; more severe acts (physical assault, explicit threats) are reported at a higher — but still minority — rate.
- **Migrant-specific finding, directly on the user's hypothesis:** the same study finds migrants have a **victimisation rate of ~10%, vs. ~2% for the non-migrant population** (benchmarked against comparable European studies) — i.e. migrants are hit ~5× more often, while reporting at a comparably low rate, so the absolute dark figure in raw incident terms is larger for migrants than the official 39.9%-foreign-victim share in §2.1 would suggest.
- **Mechanism, per the study's own police-interview component:** officers interviewed confirm that **fear that one's administrative (irregular) status will trigger a sanctioning/expulsion procedure is an active, named deterrent** keeping victims away from police stations — independently of the formal legal protection described next.

**The legal "firewall" (cortafuegos) — what's on paper vs. what the evidence suggests in practice:**
- Spanish immigration law (LOEX) explicitly **exempts victims of gender-based violence, sexual violence, trafficking, and hate crimes** from the sanctioning/expulsion procedure that would normally apply to an irregular-status person who comes forward — i.e. hate-crime victims are *nominally* covered by a reporting firewall, contrary to this doc's earlier (overly narrow) characterization of the exception as gender-violence-only.
- Despite that legal text, the ONDOD study's police-sourced finding above, plus Red Acoge's **"Denuncia In-Segura"** report, show the firewall is not functioning as a reliable guarantee in practice: Red Acoge finds only **~1 in 9 migrant crime victims (≈11%) report at all** (any crime, not hate-crimes specifically), citing fear of retaliation, distrust of institutions, and lack of information about the protection that nominally exists — i.e. awareness/enforcement gaps undercut a legal protection that on paper should apply.
- **Defensor del Pueblo** (Spanish Ombudsman) has recommended since **2004** that Interior generalize this no-sanction guarantee to *any* crime report by an irregular migrant (not just the four protected categories) — a recommendation Interior has repeatedly declined to adopt. Documented cases where the gap bites: a Honduran woman in **Xirivella** (Valencia, 2019) who went to report a gender-based assault and was instead detained with an expulsion proposal opened; a similar expulsion-proposal case in **Burjassot** after a migrant came forward to report a crime.
- **PICUM** (Platform for International Cooperation on Undocumented Migrants) independently documents Spain's absence of a *general* firewall as part of its EU-wide tracking of safe-reporting mechanisms.

**EU-wide comparator (FRA EU-MIDIS II, fielded 2015-16, Spain subsample N=1,563 — North African immigrants + Roma):** across the EU, **88% of ethnic-discrimination incidents, 90% of harassment incidents, and 72% of hate-motivated violence incidents went unreported** — the survey's authors attribute this to "resignation" and distrust of victim-protection mechanisms among minorities/immigrants. This is an EU-aggregate, not a Spain-isolated figure; a precise Spain-only reporting-rate breakout from EU-MIDIS was not pinned down in this pass (flagged below as unresolved).

### 2.3 CIS Barómetro — immigration as "main problem" perception
- Sept 2024: 30.4% named immigration as Spain's main problem — first time it topped the series, displacing unemployment.
- Maldita.es methodological flag: that survey wave placed 5 immigration/inequality questions *before* the open "main problem" question — a likely **question-order priming effect**. When later waves reverted to standard ordering, immigration mentions fell to 8th place (13.1%) within months (Nov 2024).
- Gap between *general* concern and *personal* concern: only 13.7% said immigration personally affects them, vs 30.4% naming it as a national problem.

## 3. Civil-society / international monitoring (current + historical continuity)

### 3.1 Informe Raxen — Movimiento contra la Intolerancia
Running since **1995** (founded after neo-Nazi/racist murders), Spain's longest-running NGO monitor of racist, xenophobic, antisemitic, Islamophobic, anti-Romani and ideological-hate incidents. Publishes annual national reports plus regional specials (e.g. Raxen Especial Andalucía 2025).
- Historically reports higher incident counts than the official Interior Ministry series because it tracks incidents below the criminal-hate-crime threshold and incidents the police classify under ordinary public-order offences.
- Flags the same online-to-offline pattern as OBERAXE: rising internet/social-media hate speech functioning as a radicalization on-ramp.

## 4. Case studies: trigger → hate speech → aggression, Spain

### 4.0 How the pre-internet cases spread without social media

Ca n'Anglada (1999) and El Ejido (2000) predate Spanish mass internet/mobile use entirely (smartphone penetration: zero; SMS existed but was not used as an organizing tool in either case per the sources reviewed). The amplification step that Telegram/X perform today was instead carried by three pre-digital channels, all documented in the sources below:

1. **Local/regional press and radio acting as the accelerant, not a neutral chronicler.** A Terrassa-based journalist (Oller, quoted in Público's retrospective) describes the Ca n'Anglada spiral explicitly as media-driven: "the more journalists arrived from all over the State, the more neighbours attended the demonstration" — he attributes the disproportionate national press presence partly to it being a slow summer news period. In El Ejido, the municipal station **Radio Ejido** amplified and defended the mayor's framing in real time, functioning as a local echo chamber analogous to a closed Telegram group.
2. **A political figure's rhetoric substituting for an "influencer" call-to-mobilize.** El Ejido's PP mayor, **Juan Enciso**, made repeated public statements during the crisis dehumanizing the same migrant workforce the local greenhouse economy depended on — reported in slightly different wordings across sources, all to the same effect: *"a las ocho de la mañana todos los inmigrantes son pocos, a las ocho de la tarde sobran todos"* ("at 8am there aren't enough immigrants, at 8pm they're all surplus"). This functioned as the elite-legitimization signal that GPAHE's 2025 analysis (§3.3) says licenses "ordinary" people to act — the 2000 equivalent of a far-right account's mobilization post.
3. **Physical, face-to-face assembly replacing the Telegram "cacería" call.** Both riots organized via word-of-mouth at fixed physical gathering points (town square in Ca n'Anglada; convoys of vehicles and gathering at the agricultural settlements/cortijos in El Ejido) rather than an online call — meaning mobilization was geographically constrained to the local population (plus some regional press-drawn observers), unlike Torre Pacheco 2025 where Telegram pulled in participants from other provinces within hours. This is the single biggest structural difference vs. the social-media-era cases: **reach and speed were bounded by physical proximity and broadcast-media cycles (hours/days) in 1999-2000, vs. instantaneous and nationwide in 2025.**

### 4.1 Ca n'Anglada, Terrassa — July 1999 (precedent)
Stabbing/brawl trigger (14 July 1999) → next-day neighbourhood protest that grew as press arrived → demonstration of **~2,000 of the neighbourhood's ~14,000 residents** → ended in assault on a young Moroccan man, stoning of Maghrebi homes, and an attempted assault on the local mosque; ~1,000 people attended a subsequent counter anti-racist rally. Widely cited as the first major xenophobic riot of this kind in democratic Spain, pre-dating El Ejido by ~7 months.

### 4.2 El Ejido, Almería — February 2000 (landmark historical case)
**Trigger:** murder of two Spanish residents (one stoned, one knifed) by a Moroccan agricultural day-laborer on 22 Jan 2000, on top of a third recent murder attributed to a different migrant.
**Escalation:** three days of organized arson and assault — homes, vehicles, halal butchers, a mosque and migrant shacks burned, attackers armed with iron bars/bats — explicitly targeting the ~one-third-foreign agricultural workforce as a group, not the individual perpetrator.
**Structural backdrop:** academic analyses (Scielo/Dialnet, cited below) tie the explosion to pre-existing labour-market segregation (Moroccan workers had structurally replaced family labour in El Ejido's greenhouse economy from 1985) — i.e. economic/social segregation as the substrate onto which the crime narrative landed.
**Response:** >500 police deployed (Aznar government); unrest ended when foreign workers called a strike.

### 4.3 Torre Pacheco, Murcia — July 2025 (current landmark case)
**Trigger:** assault on a 68-year-old resident on 9 July 2025; three men of North African origin arrested.
**Disinformation amplification (documented by Maldita.es):**
- A "suspect poster" naming/picturing 5 men as the attackers circulated virally — confirmed false by the Interior Ministry; those pictured had no connection to the case.
- A video purporting to show the beating, unrelated to Torre Pacheco, became the single most-shared piece of misinformation on X — Maldita tracked 50 posts with >1.6M views as of 18 July, **none carrying a Community Note**.
- A Russian state-linked disinformation network ("Pravda") pushed ~300 posts amplifying the unrest 10-15 July.
**Offline mobilization:** organizers used Telegram to call "cacerías" (hunts) against migrants; hundreds of far-right-aligned young men traveled in from other provinces over about a week; street battles, attacks on people and businesses of Moroccan origin; the Telegram group used to coordinate violence was eventually shut down by the platform.
**Outcome:** ~300 people registered/investigated by police as of 24 July 2025; large Guardia Civil/local police deployment to halt further unrest.
**Direct discourse→speech link, quantified:** OBERAXE's special report (24 July 2025) documents hate speech "overflowing" social media in direct response to the events; the same Jul-Aug 2024 OBERAXE finding (§1) — over half of hate speech being disinformation-based — is consistent with what Maldita.es independently documented for this specific case.
**Stated intent to replicate:** Spanish far-right YouTube figures had, in summer 2024 (a year prior), explicitly cited the English anti-migrant riots as a template to import to Spain — suggesting Torre Pacheco was not spontaneous but sat on a year of rhetorical priming.

## 5. What the "migrant crime" framing gets wrong, per the debunking literature

Directly relevant because the trigger step (§1) leans on a perceived migration↔crime link:
- A 2012 Universidad Carlos III de Madrid study found **no strong correlation** between immigration levels and crime rates in Spain.
- Spain's overall crime rate fell from 49.4/1,000 inhabitants (2005) to ~40.6 today even as the foreign-resident population roughly doubled to ~7 million by 2025 (el diario / INE-MIR synthesis) — i.e. the topline trend runs opposite to the "more migration → more crime" narrative, even as **hate crimes specifically** have risen.
- The large majority of those *investigated/detained for hate crimes themselves* are Spanish nationals (§2.1) — the "ordinary, legitimized actor" pattern GPAHE describes, not organized foreign criminal actors.

This section is the direct mirror of this repo's own V14 invariant (relative vs. absolute framing) and connects to the in-progress T26-T29 migrant-crime-rate work: that work is computing the *absolute* base rates that this discourse-debunking literature argues are being misrepresented in relative/anecdotal form.
