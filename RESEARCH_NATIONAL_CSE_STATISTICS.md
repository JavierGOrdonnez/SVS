# National CSE Statistics: The Scale Problem

**Purpose**: Put grooming gangs in context of total child sexual abuse  
**Data Sources**: ONS, NSPCC, CSA Centre, VKPP 2023-2024  
**Branch**: `research/uk-grooming-policy-analysis`

---

## The Scale of Child Sexual Abuse in the UK

### Annual Recorded Cases

- **2024**: 122,768 child sexual abuse and exploitation (CSAE) offences recorded
- **2023**: 115,489 CSAE offences recorded
- **2022/23**: ~87,000 sexual offences against children recorded

**But**: Estimated 500,000 children per year experience child sexual abuse of any kind in the UK, with only ~1 in 5 reported.

### Victims Affected (Annual Assessment Data)

- **29,560 children** assessed as affected by child sexual abuse (2025)
- **12,120 children** assessed as affected by child sexual exploitation (2025)

### Long-Term Prevalence

[According to Crime Survey for England and Wales](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/crimeandjustice/bulletins/abuseduringchildhoodinenglandandwales/march2024):
- **9.1% of UK adults** (18+) experienced sexual abuse as a child
- **13.9% of women** experienced childhood sexual abuse
- **4.1% of men** experienced childhood sexual abuse

## The Breakdown: What Types of Abuse?

### Group-Based CSE vs. Individual Perpetrators

**Critical finding**: [Group-based CSE accounts for only 4.6% of all CSAE in 2023](https://www.vkpp.org.uk/assets/Totality-year-2-report-National-Analysis-of-Police-Recorded-CSAE-Crimes-Report-2023_compressed.pdf)

| Type | Offences | % of Total |
|------|----------|-----------|
| Group-based contact offences | 3.7% | |
| Group-based non-contact offences | ~1% | |
| **Total group-based** | **4.6%** | |
| **Individual perpetrators** | **95.4%** | |

**What this means**: The "grooming gang" issue that dominates the narrative represents less than 5% of all reported child sexual abuse. 95% is perpetrated by individuals.

### Within Group-Based Offences: The Family Factor

Of 4,228 group-based offences in 2023:

- **Family abuse**: 1,125 offences (26.6%)
- **Sexual exploitation (organized gangs)**: 717 offences (17.0%)
- **Other group-based**: Remaining 1,386 offences (56.4%)

**Key point**: Even within "group-based" CSE, nearly 27% involves family members. When we talk about "grooming gangs," we're talking about 17% of group-based cases, which is 17% of 4.6% of total CSE = **0.78% of all reported CSE**.

## Perpetrator Characteristics

### Perpetrator Type: Who Abuses Children?

**Where abuse occurs**:
- **By people victims know**: ~63-70% of cases
  - Friends/acquaintances: 37%
  - Family members: Significant proportion (26-32% overall)
  - Strangers: 30%

**Contact vs. non-contact abuse**:
- **Contact abuse**: 80% perpetrated by friends and family members
- **Non-contact abuse**: Strangers 34.7%, friends/acquaintances 30.8%

**Perpetrator demographics**:
- **Gender**: 82% male (when gender recorded)
- **Age**: 50% of CSAE offences committed by children aged 10-17 years
- **Adult perpetrators**: Most often aged 18+ (69.8%)

### Perpetrator Ethnicity (Where Recorded)

[2025 Centre of Expertise on Child Sexual Abuse data](https://www.csacentre.org.uk/app/uploads/2025/03/Child-sexual-abuse-in-2023-24-Trends-in-official-data.pdf) (ethnicity recorded in 69% of cases):

| Ethnicity | Percentage |
|-----------|-----------|
| White | 90% |
| Asian | 5% |
| Black | 2% |
| Mixed | 2% |
| Other | 1% |

**Critical context**: This is across ALL CSE, not just group-based. And ethnicity is missing for 31% of cases.

## The Online Abuse Trend

- **2024**: 26% increase in CSAE with online footprint
- **Current**: 42% of all CSAE now has an online component

**Implication**: The fastest-growing category of CSA has an online element—not specific to in-person gangs.

## The Victim Profile

### Victim Demographics

- **Gender**: 78% female, 22% male
- **Age**: Most victims aged 10-19 years
- **Relationship to perpetrator**: Majority know the perpetrator
- **Vulnerability factors**:
  - In care system
  - Disengaged from education
  - Multiple missing episodes
  - Poverty/socioeconomic marginalization
  - Lack of trusted adults

### Re-Victimization

- **CSE re-referral rate**: 29% in 2023
- Suggests persistent vulnerability to repeated harm despite intervention

## Putting "Grooming Gangs" in Context

### The Mathematical Reality

Starting with total reported CSE: **~115,000 offences**

Work down to organized gang CSE:
1. Group-based CSE: 4.6% = **5,290 offences**
2. Of those, organized sexual exploitation (not family): ~17% = **900 offences**
3. Of those, the high-profile cases with ethnic component: ???

**The "grooming gang" cases that dominate national discourse likely represent <1% of all reported CSE.**

### Under-Reporting Problem

- **Reported**: ~115,000 offences annually
- **Estimated actual**: ~500,000 children abused annually
- **Reporting rate**: ~20%

**This means**: For every grooming gang case that gets media attention and policy focus, there are:
- 19 cases not reported at all
- 20 individual perpetrator cases for every group-based case
- Family abuse cases making up a quarter of what IS reported

## What The Data Shows About Perpetrator Origins

### Ethnicity of All CSE Perpetrators

90% are white (where ethnicity recorded). This includes:
- Family perpetrators (mostly white)
- Individual perpetrators (mostly white)
- Group-based perpetrators (mix, but majority still white by numbers)

### The Asian Perpetrator Fraction

5% of all recorded CSE involves Asian perpetrators. This includes:
- Individuals
- Group-based gangs
- Family abuse

**In absolute terms**: Of 115,000 CSE offences, approximately 5,750 involve Asian perpetrators (rough estimate). Of those, group-based organized cases would be a small fraction.

### Comparison to Population

- **Asian population in UK**: 7.5%
- **Asian CSE perpetrators** (where ethnicity recorded): 5%
- **Proportional representation**: Slightly below population proportion nationally

(Note: Regional variations exist—Greater Manchester, South Yorkshire, West Yorkshire show different patterns—but national is proportional or below.)

## The Policy Implication Question

If you're considering policy responses to prevent CSE, the data suggests:

### What You'd Be Targeting

**If focus is on migration/origin policy**:
- You're targeting 5% of perpetrators (Asian perpetrators across all categories)
- Of which, an unknown but small fraction are recent migrants
- To prevent organized gang CSE specifically, you're targeting <1% of total CSE
- While 95% of CSE is perpetrated by individuals (unaffected by migration policy)
- And 26% of what IS reported involves family (also unaffected by migration policy)
- And 50% of recorded offences are committed by children (also unaffected by adult migration policy)

### What You're NOT Addressing

- 95% of CSE perpetrators (individuals)
- 26% of group-based offences (family abuse)
- 80% of contact abuse (friends and family)
- Unreported abuse (80% of estimated total)
- Child-perpetrators (50% of recorded offences)
- Online abuse (growing 26% annually)

---

## Data Quality Issues

### What's Missing

1. **Ethnicity data**: Missing for 31% of cases (2025 report) to 61% of cases (2024 NPCC data)
2. **Perpetrator-victim relationship**: Not always recorded
3. **Organized vs. unorganized**: Not consistently distinguished
4. **Immigration status**: Virtually never recorded (so can't distinguish recent migrants from long-residents)
5. **Underlying causation**: Institutional failure vs. cultural factors not measured

### Implications for Policy

You cannot effectively target migration policy when:
- You don't know immigration status of perpetrators
- You don't know whether recent migrants or long-residents
- You don't know whether UK-born children of immigrants
- Most data doesn't even have ethnicity recorded

## The Prevention Research Finding

From [CSA Centre analysis](https://www.csacentre.org.uk/app/uploads/2025/03/Child-sexual-abuse-in-2023-24-Trends-in-official-data.pdf) and [Casey Report](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/685559d05225e4ed0bf3ce54/National_Audit_on_Group-based_Child_Sexual_Exploitation_and_Abuse.pdf):

**What actually prevents CSE across all categories**:
- Institutional safeguarding (applies to all victims)
- Early intervention for vulnerable children (applies regardless of perpetrator origin)
- Inter-agency communication (applies to all cases)
- Victim support and belief (applies to all cases)
- Online safety measures (applies to online abuse—fastest growing category)

**What migration policy would address**:
- Theoretically: Recent migrants only
- In practice: Unknown, since migration status not recorded
- Scope: 5% of recorded perpetrators (Asian), unknown fraction of those being recent migrants
- Categories it wouldn't touch: Individual perpetrators (95%), family abuse (26%), children perpetrators (50%)

---

## Summary: The Context Problem

The "grooming gang" debate, by dominating policy attention, risks:

1. **Misallocating resources**: Focusing on <1% of CSE problem
2. **Ignoring the majority**: 95% of CSE is individual perpetrators
3. **Neglecting family abuse**: 26% of reported cases involve family
4. **Ignoring unreported abuse**: 80% goes unreported entirely
5. **Missing child perpetrators**: 50% of offences are by children
6. **Overlooking online growth**: 26% increase in 2024, now 42% of all offences
7. **Relying on incomplete data**: 31-61% missing ethnicity data; immigration status essentially never recorded

**The policy question**: Even if migration policy could somehow target the Asian perpetrator fraction (5% of total), would that be the highest-impact use of child protection resources?
