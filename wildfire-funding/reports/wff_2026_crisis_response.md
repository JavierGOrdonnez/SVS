## Madrid (Comunidad de Madrid) — INFOMA 2026

**RE-AUDIT NOTE (2026-07-29)**: INFOMA's published 6,110 figure is the ENTIRE
civil-protection apparatus (general Bomberos urban+forest, Protección Civil volunteers,
forest agents, Madrid 112). Wildfire-DEDICATED forces are much smaller:
~419-450 TRAGSA-managed bomberos forestales (year-round), 61 public-sector bomberos
forestales (currently INACTIVE due to labor dispute), ~220 seasonal hires. Total
dedicated: ~700-730, minus 61 inactive. The 10 helicopters serve the entire INFOMA,
not exclusively wildfire. See `data/raw/wff_operational_resources.csv` row 15 (`confidence=unverified`)
for full breakdown. The Comunidad's INFOMA announcement figures are preserved below
as-published for reference, but **do not represent wildfire-dedicated strength**.

### Budget
- **€52.7M** for 2026 wildfire season (+3.5% vs 2025). Madrid claims highest per-hectare investment in Europe.
- Since 2019: total investment +38%, INFOMA personnel +23.5%.
- Plan Forestal 2026-2030: €160M over 5 years, ~€10M+/year for prevention specifically (€48.5M for cortafuegos/rupture areas).
- **Structural finding (earlier pass)**: Madrid's budget does NOT isolate wildfire spending into its own program—it's split across program 456A (Biodiversidad, 51.4M) and 134A (Emergencias, 280M). The INFOMA 52.7M figure is a cross-program estimate. TRAGSA entrustment (€107M, 2022-2025, program 134A/subconcepto 22706) confirmed via primary document.

### Personnel — as published by Comunidad (INFOMA total, not wildfire-dedicated)
- **6,110** professionals + volunteers (+2.3% vs 5,970 in 2025)
  - 1,680 Bomberos (general corps, urban+forest)
  - 350 Agentes Forestales
  - 3,420 Protección Civil voluntarios (113 grupos locales)
  - 180 Madrid 112 professionals
  - 571 daily extinction personnel (all services combined)
- Wildfire-dedicated (corrected): ~419-450 TRAGSA-managed bomberos forestales (year-round);
  61 public-sector bomberos forestales (INACTIVE); ~220 seasonal hires. Total ~700-730.
- 267 new bomberos forestales being recruited (September 2026 — likely includes the ~220
  seasonal hires plus permanent replacements; Comunidad's own figure, scope not independently verified)

### Aircraft
- **10 helicopters** (8 helipads), contracted to SKY HELICÓPTEROS, ELIANCE, PEGASUS
- Grupo Especial de Drones (thermal imaging)

### Vehicles & infrastructure
- 27 heavy pumps (bombas pesadas)
- 8 water tanker trucks (camiones nodriza)
- 49 4x4 transport vehicles
- 2 mechanized units
- 22 fire stations, 25 brigade stations, 17 heavy-pump stations
- 38 watchtowers, 4 cameras

### Currently deployed to the fire (Ayuso, 28 July)
- 300 bomberos forestales + agentes forestales
- 50 ground vehicles
- 10 aircraft

### Key investigative findings
- **TSJM ruling (2026-01-13)**: Court confirmed ~€40M (2019-2023) of legally earmarked fire-service insurance surcharge funds diverted to vehicle leasing/current expenses instead of capital investment. Firefighters' union explicitly links this to current capacity gaps (40+ vehicles out of service, and zero dedicated firetrucks for forest-dedicated units in 19 parks per EL PAÍS 2026-07-26).
- **TRAGSA entrustment** (the strongest primary-sourced finding in the entire project): €107M, 2022-2025, assigned to program 134A/subconcepto 22706—a generically labeled line. The original press headlines cited "~2,100 forest-brigade personnel" but re-audit (2026-07-29) shows actual TRAGSA-managed bomberos forestales = ~419-450 year-round. The TRAGSA line is real; the 2,100 figure conflated the entire general Bomberos corps (1,680) with the dedicated forest brigade.

## Castilla y León (Ávila) — INFOCAL 2026

### Budget
- **€169.63M** total for 2026 (Junta's own Cortes testimony, Jan 2026):
  - €151M chapters VI+VII (investment programs)
  - €16M personnel (after converting fijo-discontinuo to full-year)
  - €2.63M Consejo Comarcal del Bierzo (5 cuadrillas)
- Evolution: 94.5M → 135M (proposal) → 169.6M (approved modifications) → ~160M (June press summary, operativo-only scope)
- Transition to Tragsa for 49 of 111 ground teams costs an additional €40M/year (ramp from 54M to 101M by 2029)
- **NOTE**: Castilla y León's 2026 budgets are prorogued from 2024 with modifications (C7). The earlier 104.3M presupuestado figure (2025, existing dataset) was the narrower 'Programas de Prevención y Extinción' line only.

### Personnel
- **5,075** professionals (+355 vs 2025)
  - 837 fijos-discontinuos converted to fijos (Dec 2025)
  - 129 ground teams (49 transitioning to Tragsa)
  - 24 helitransported teams (ELIF)
  - 16 night teams (5 months duration)
  - 9 UBA (Unidad de Brigada y Autobomba) units
  - 163 watchtower posts

### Aircraft
- **26 helicopters** + **1 ACT** (air cargo truck, MITECO)
- MITECO contribution: 6 helis, 2x 5,000L amphibious, 2x 3,500L amphibious, 1 ACO
- Plan: 27 aircraft in 2026, 33 in 2027 (4 new rapid-intervention helitransported teams)
- Ávila helibases: El Barco (ELIF), Piedralaves (ELIF), Cebreros (ELIF), Puerto El Pico (BRIF B)

### Vehicles & infrastructure
- 94 own fire trucks (autobombas, >3,000L tanks); 28 with night crews year-round
- 30 reserve trucks (2025 campaign carryover)
- 105 in collaboration agreements (municipalities/diputaciones)
- 38 pickups with water tanks
- 27 new trucks being acquired (2026)
- 20 bulldozer teams (preventive, year-round) + 20 more in EPA (high danger)
- 2+ extra water tankers (>25,000L each)
- 15 PMA vehicles, 15 drones, 322 surveillance cameras

### Currently deployed to Burgohondo fire (INFOCAL, 26 July)
- **109 medios total**: 95 terrestrial, 14 aerial
- **560 professionals** (INFOCAL specific)
- 16 ground teams, 12 helitransported teams
- 23 autobombas, 7 bulldozers
- Plus MITECO reinforcement and convoys from Galicia, Madrid, Extremadura, Murcia, Asturias, Andalucía, Portugal
- **3,000+ total professionals** across all three active fronts (Diario de León, 28 July)

### Key investigative findings
- **Chronic under-delivery**: EL PAÍS (2026-06-25) documented that promised helicopter
  reinforcements had not arrived at El Barco de Ávila, Pradoluengo (Burgos), Garray
  (Soria), Villaeles (Palencia) at campaign start. "Cuadrillas incompletas, falta de
  helicópteros, camiones parados porque no hay conductores."
- **State/Junta asymmetry**: In June León fires, MITECO deployed 12 aircraft vs the
  Junta's 4 (elDiario.es, 2026-06-25). Same pattern repeats in the current Ávila crisis:
  the national government carries a disproportionate share of the aerial response.
- **Mid-2025 execution**: 48.5% of the 104.3M "Programas de Prevención y Extinción"
  budget was executed as of 1 June 2025 (pre-fire-season snapshot). The 2025 fire
  season (140,000+ ha in CyL) almost certainly drove mid-year extraordinary credits,
  but the year-end liquidado figure is not yet published.

The structural pattern: prevention was halved after the 2008 crisis and never recovered.
EU funds earmarked for forest management have a 26% execution rate even as the worst
fire season on record unfolds. Both Madrid and CyL increased nominal budgets in 2026,
but from a low base—and the execution gap (budgeted vs actually spent, especially on
prevention) remains the critical unknown.

## Key unknowns for future work

1. **CyL liquidado 2026**: The 169.63M presupuestado vs eventual execution—especially
   for prevention lines—will only be known in 2027-2028 (Intervención General, 1-2yr
   lag).
2. **Madrid liquidado**: Same gap. The TSJM ruling proves a 5-year diversion pattern
   on one specific funding stream; whether total fire-program execution is also below
   budget is not tracked in public data at the needed granularity.
3. **Prevention/extinction split**: Neither Madrid nor CyL publishes a clean split.
   The CyL budget (169.63M) is disclosed as total fire-fighting; the prevention portion
   is not itemized. Madrid's 52.7M INFOMA is the closest thing to a prevention figure,
   but includes extinction operations.
4. **EGIF gastos coverage**: Madrid's extinction-cost reporting to EGIF stopped in 1991;
   Castilla y León's in 2014. The Civio dataset captures what CCAAs choose to report,
   not the full picture.
5. **Eu funds execution**: Whether the 295M unmobilized EU funds can still reach the
   ground before the NextGen deadline, and whether any portion is redirected to the
   2026 emergency response.
