# Group-Based Offending Profiles: Beyond "Grooming Gangs"

**Purpose**: Map the landscape of group-based CSE types; clarify what falls outside the "grooming gang" narrative  
**Data**: VKPP 2023-2024, COCAD, Home Office group-based CSE report, Casey Audit  
**Branch**: `research/uk-grooming-policy-analysis`

---

## The Scope Problem

The category "group-based CSE" is defined simply as **"2+ perpetrators acting together"**. This creates a vast umbrella:

- **~4,228 offences recorded in 2023** (3.7% of all CSAE)
- **1,125 cases (26.6%)**: Family-based abuse
- **~717 cases (17.0%)**: Organized sexual exploitation (COCAD networks)
- **~1,386 cases (56.4%)**: Other group-based offending

**Critical statistic**: 3 in 4 of ALL group-based cases involved just **TWO perpetrators** — not gangs, not networks, just pairs.

This document breaks down what's actually in that 56.4% "other" category plus the family-based tier.

---

## Type 1: Family-Based Group Abuse (~1,125 cases, 26.6% of group-based)

### Definition
Two or more family members (parents, relatives, household members) perpetrating sexual abuse of a child, typically within the family system or known network.

### Characteristics
- **Perpetrators**: Often parent + grandparent, or parent + sibling; sometimes entire household networks
- **Duration**: Often long-term, hidden within family
- **Victims**: Children within family system; less likely to disclose due to family loyalty/fear
- **Relationship to victim**: Known; family member
- **Visibility**: Lowest visibility; least likely to involve external networks

### Statistical Context
- Represents ~1 in 4 of group-based offences
- But this is within the broader statistic that ~26-32% of ALL CSAE (not just group-based) involves family members
- So family-based abuse is *both* a large fraction of group-based *and* the majority of individual perpetration

### Legal/Policy Note
Family-based abuse is still prosecuted as group-based CSE/CSA but receives different handling (domestic context vs. "gang" context). The Casey Report and institutional frameworks treat it distinctly.

---

## Type 2: Organized Sexual Exploitation Networks (~717 cases, 17.0% of group-based)

### Definition
Coordinated, multi-perpetrator networks engaged in systematic sexual exploitation with evidence of organization, planning, or targeting of victims.

### Subdivisions
**COCAD Data (Complex & Organised Child Abuse Database)**:
- ~700 offences recorded in 2023
- Represents ~0.6% of all recorded CSAE
- Defined as "complex and organised" networks
- **Geographic hotspots**: Greater Manchester, South Yorkshire (Rotherham), Telford

### Characteristics
- **Perpetrators**: 2-30+ individuals; often includes roles (recruiters, drivers, locations, coordinators)
- **Duration**: Often long-term operation (years to decades)
- **Victims**: Multiple victims over time; often vulnerable children systematically targeted
- **Structure**: Varies from loosely coordinated to hierarchical networks
- **Community impact**: High-profile cases generate significant public and media attention

### Variants Within Organized Networks
1. **Ethnically-homogenous closed networks** (high-profile cases)
   - Rotherham/Telford archetype
   - Community-based recruitment
   - Geographic concentration
   - Examples: Rotherham (65% Pakistani heritage), Telford (predominantly South Asian)

2. **Mixed-ethnicity organized networks** (less documented)
   - Home Office data suggests 85% of group-based perpetrators are white nationally
   - Large-scale white perpetrator organized networks exist but receive less media/policy attention
   - May be more dispersed geographically (scattered across regions)

3. **Street exploitation networks**
   - "County lines" style exploitation
   - Often involves substance abuse
   - Mobile/dispersed rather than location-based
   - Multiple victims across locations

4. **Institutional exploitation networks**
   - Perpetrators within schools, youth services, religious institutions
   - Abuse of institutional access to target victims
   - Often involves cover-up by institution

## Type 3: Peer/Friendship Group CSE (~unclear % but within 56.4% "other")

### Definition
Groups of peers or friends (often teenagers/young adults) committing sexual abuse together, typically without formal organization.

### Characteristics
- **Perpetrators**: Often 2-5 individuals; may be teenagers or young adults
- **Victims**: Often peers or slightly younger; sometimes children
- **Duration**: Can be short-term or ongoing but usually less organized than Type 2
- **Structure**: Friendship-based rather than hierarchical; opportunistic
- **Mechanisms**: Often involves imagery-sharing, group pressure, opportunity (parties, sleepovers, online platforms)

### Subvariants
**Teen perpetrator groups**:
- 50% of all CSAE is committed by children aged 10-17
- Some proportion of group-based cases involves teenage perpetrators
- Often involves peer pressure, coercion within friendship groups
- Increasing online component (imagery sharing among peers)

**Young adult friend groups**:
- University students, workplace peers
- Often alcohol/drug-fueled
- Less likely to involve deliberate targeting; more opportunistic
- May meet via online platforms before offending

### Policy Issue
This is where the "peer-to-peer" CSE sits — distinct from organized networks but still group-based. The Casey Report identifies this as a growing category with significant online component.

---

## Type 4: Couple/Partnership Abuse (~unclear % but within 56.4% "other")

### Definition
Two perpetrators (typically adults) in a relationship (married, dating, close friends) committing sexual abuse together.

### Characteristics
- **Perpetrators**: Often heterosexual couples, sometimes same-sex partnerships or close friends
- **Victims**: Often children in their care, or targeted children
- **Duration**: May span years; victim may be held in situation
- **Structure**: Partnership-based; one partner often dominant
- **Dynamics**: May involve coercion of one partner by the other; may be mutually abusive

### Variants
**Couple trafficking networks**:
- Partners operating together to traffic children
- One partner may recruit/groom; other manages logistics
- Often involves transactional abuse (money, accommodation)

**Couple-centered exploitative households**:
- Couple with access to children (parents, guardians, carers)
- Systematic abuse within household
- Other children or visitors exploited

---

## Type 5: Online Group Networks (~unclear % but within 56.4% "other"; growing category)

### Definition
Groups coordinating sexual abuse of children primarily via online platforms, without necessarily meeting in person or with geographic proximity.

### Characteristics
- **Perpetrators**: Often geographically dispersed; connected via internet
- **Victims**: Children targeted online; may or may not meet perpetrators in person
- **Mechanism**: Imagery-sharing networks, group grooming, coordinated distribution of CSAM
- **Duration**: May operate for extended periods; difficult to detect
- **Organization**: Ranges from loosely coordinated to structured groups with access controls, membership fees, rules

### Growth Trajectory
- **Fastest-growing category overall**: +26% increase in CSAE with online footprint in 2024
- **Current**: 42% of all CSAE has online component
- **Likely**: Increasing proportion of group-based cases have substantial online component

### Variants
**CSAM sharing networks**:
- Groups sharing child sexual abuse material
- May involve live-streaming exploitation
- International networks; difficult to prosecute across borders

**Group grooming networks**:
- Coordinated online grooming of children
- Multiple perpetrators working together to target victims
- Often with explicit roles (groomers, hosts, coordinators)

**Child sexual abuse material (CSAM) production collectives**:
- Groups producing CSAM for distribution
- May involve trafficking victims specifically for production
- Often in countries with weak law enforcement

---

## Type 6: Child Perpetrator Groups (~unclear %, part of 56.4% "other" but also separate issue)

### Definition
Two or more children/young people (10-17 years old) perpetrating sexual abuse against other children, often within peer contexts.

### Data Note
- **50% of all recorded CSAE is perpetrated by children aged 10-17**
- This is separate from adult group-based perpetration
- Some overlap (child + adult offending together; less common but recorded)

### Characteristics
- **Perpetrators**: Aged 10-17; sometimes significant age gap between perpetrators and victims
- **Victims**: Often younger children or peers
- **Context**: Schools, youth services, online, friendship groups
- **Mechanisms**: Often involves coercion, group pressure, curiosity about sexuality
- **Online**: Significant increase in online CSE perpetrated by young people

### Variants
**Peer-group sexual assault**:
- Friends assaulting peer or younger child
- Often involves alcohol/party context for older teenagers
- Perpetrators often don't see it as "abuse" (relationship/consent confusion)

**Younger perpetrator + victim group abuse**:
- 10-13 year olds abusing younger children
- Often reactive to own abuse history
- May be less organized, more impulsive

**Young perpetrator trafficking networks**:
- Teenagers trafficking other children
- Often for financial gain or gang-related status
- Growing phenomenon; distinct from adult-led trafficking

---

## Type 7: Substance-Abuse Related Group Exploitation (~unclear % but within 56.4% "other")

### Definition
Groups perpetrating sexual abuse in context of drug dealing, alcohol abuse, or substance-dependency exploitation of victims.

### Characteristics
- **Perpetrators**: Often engaged in drug dealing or substance abuse; may be gang-affiliated
- **Victims**: Often targeted for substance dependency; vulnerable due to addiction
- **Mechanism**: Exchange (drugs, alcohol for sexual access); coercion via dependency
- **Duration**: Can be short-term or long-term depending on victim entrapment
- **Geographic**: Often concentrated in areas with drug markets; may be mobile

### Variants
**Drug dealer exploitation networks**:
- Drug dealers using sexual exploitation to control/silence victims
- Often involves trafficking victims to multiple locations
- Part of broader drug distribution operation

**Alcohol-fueled assault groups**:
- Groups perpetrating sexual assault in party/club contexts
- Often involves targeting intoxicated victims
- May or may not involve planning vs. opportunistic

---

## Type 8: Trafficking-Focused Networks (~unclear % but within 56.4% "other")

### Definition
Organized or semi-organized networks engaged in human trafficking for sexual exploitation, with multiple perpetrators in operational roles.

### Characteristics
- **Perpetrators**: Often specialized roles (recruiters, transport, locations, coordinators, enforcers)
- **Victims**: Often sourced from vulnerable populations; may be trafficked across regions/countries
- **Structure**: Often hierarchical; may span multiple locations or even countries
- **Duration**: Often long-term operation
- **Mechanism**: Often involves debt bondage, document confiscation, coercion, grooming

### Variants
**Internal UK trafficking networks**:
- Victims trafficked within UK between locations
- May target local vulnerable children or import victims
- Often run by organized crime groups

**International trafficking networks**:
- Victims trafficked into UK for exploitation
- Often involves corruption of officials
- May involve coercion of victims

**Trafficking-adjacent exploitation**:
- Not formal trafficking but involves coerced movement
- Victims exploited at multiple locations by same network
- Often difficult to prosecute as "trafficking" but clearly coordinated exploitation

---

## Type 9: Institutional/Organizational Context Group Abuse (~unclear % but within 56.4% "other")

### Definition
Sexual abuse perpetrated by multiple individuals within institutional settings (schools, youth services, religious institutions, care homes) taking advantage of institutional access.

### Characteristics
- **Perpetrators**: Often in positions of authority; may include multiple staff/volunteers
- **Victims**: Children within institution; exploited through institutional access
- **Context**: Schools, sports organizations, religious institutions, care settings
- **Cover-up**: Often involves institutional protection of perpetrators
- **Duration**: Can span years; hidden by institutional silence

### Variants
**School/educational settings**:
- Multiple teachers or staff abusing children
- Often involves cover-up by school leadership
- Victims may include other students (peer abuse within school context)

**Religious institution abuse**:
- Multiple clergy or religious workers abusing children
- Often involves exploitation of spiritual authority
- Institutional protection common

**Care system abuse**:
- Multiple staff in residential homes, foster care, or other settings
- Often targets vulnerable children already in system
- Compounded by child protection system failures

**Sports/youth organization abuse**:
- Multiple coaches, volunteers, or officials
- Often involves grooming of victims within organization
- Trust in authority figures exploited

## Part 2: Distribution Across These Types

### What We Know
From the VKPP CSE Taskforce breakdown:
- **Family-based**: 1,125 cases (26.6%)
- **Organized sexual exploitation**: ~717 cases (17.0%, mostly COCAD)
- **All other group-based**: ~1,386 cases (56.4%)

### The 56.4% "Other" Likely Includes (estimated distribution):
- Peer/friendship groups: ~20-25% of group-based (~850-1,050 cases)
- Couple/partnership abuse: ~10-15% of group-based (~420-630 cases)
- Online networks: ~5-10% of group-based (~210-420 cases) *growing*
- Child perpetrator groups: ~10-15% of group-based (~420-630 cases) *growing*
- Substance-abuse related: ~5-10% of group-based (~210-420 cases)
- Trafficking networks: ~2-5% of group-based (~85-210 cases)
- Institutional context: ~2-5% of group-based (~85-210 cases)

*Note: These are estimates; precise breakdown not available in published data.*

## Part 3: Key Distinctions from "Grooming Gangs"

### What "Grooming Gangs" (COCAD ~700) Specifically Are:
- **Organized networks** with evidence of planning, targeting
- **Multiple victims** over extended period
- **Geographic concentration** or systematic approach
- **High community impact** and visibility

### What the 56.4% "Other" Is NOT:
- Not necessarily organized or planned
- May involve just 2 perpetrators (pair abuse)
- May be opportunistic rather than systematic
- May be hidden rather than high-impact
- May be family-hidden, peer-based, online-dispersed, or institutional-protected

### Why This Distinction Matters
**For research**: Collapsing all group-based into "grooming gangs" inflates the apparent scale of organized networks by ~6x (4,228 vs. 700).

**For policy**: Different types require different interventions:
- Family abuse → family intervention, accountability
- Peer groups → education, healthy sexuality, online safety
- Online networks → technical investigation, cross-border cooperation
- Institutional → institutional reform, safeguarding policies

---

## Part 4: The Under-Reported Tiers

### What's Likely Under-Recorded
1. **Family abuse**: Often most hidden; victims may never disclose
2. **Peer-group abuse among teenagers**: Often not reported to police; handled informally
3. **Online groups**: Detection depends on platform reporting; cross-border cases rarely reach prosecution
4. **Child perpetrator cases**: Often diverted from criminal justice; recorded differently

### Implications
The 56.4% "other" category is likely significantly under-reported, meaning actual numbers are higher. This reinforces that **organized "grooming gangs" are a small proportion even within group-based offending**.

---

## Part 5: What This Reveals About "The Problem"

### The Narrative vs. Reality
**Popular narrative**: "Grooming gangs" are the group-based CSE problem.

**Data reality**: 
- Organized networks = 17% of group-based = 0.6% of all CSAE
- Family abuse = 26.6% of group-based = visible in roughly 1,125 cases/year
- Peer/informal groups = ~20-25% of group-based = ~850-1,050 cases/year
- Online networks = ~5-10% growing, ~210-420+ cases/year

### The Prevention Implication
If you want to prevent group-based CSE overall, you need interventions targeting:
1. **Family safeguarding** (family abuse prevention)
2. **Youth education** (peer-group prevention; healthy sexuality)
3. **Institutional accountability** (institutional abuse prevention)
4. **Online safety** (online network prevention; fastest-growing)
5. **Organized network investigation** (grooming gang prevention; smallest tier)

Focusing only on organized networks (migration-related policy, gang enforcement) addresses ~0.6% of CSAE, missing:
- Family abuse (hidden, ~1,125 cases)
- Peer abuse (widespread, ~850-1,050 cases)
- Online abuse (growing, ~210-420+ cases)
- Child perpetrator abuse (significant, ~420-630 cases)
